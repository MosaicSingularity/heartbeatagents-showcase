The lag is the point. Diet and stress move the gut within hours; the circulatory signature trails by about a day; the inflammatory tone trails by two to three. Scrub slowly and watch the body change after the input, not with it.

What this is. An educational visualization of mechanisms described in peer-reviewed literature. Every mechanism is footnoted; where the underlying work is in animals it is marked. Sample data is synthetic and illustrative. Nothing here is diagnostic, predictive of an individual, or medical advice — and the relationships it shows are correlations, not causes.
Reading the language. “Moves with”, “tracks”, “co-occurs”, “is associated with”. The literature establishes mechanism and association; it rarely establishes that one thing causes the other in a person.

Typical log: 27 seconds. It is the only part of this that depends on you.

What thirty seconds buys you

A daily gut score and a daily mood score, on the same day, for weeks. That is enough to estimate your offset — the number of days between a change in your gut and a change in how you feel.

  • Day 1–14: too little data to say anything. It stays quiet.
  • Day 15–45: a trend starts to be visible, labelled uncertain.
  • Day 45+: your own lag, your own variance, your own baseline.
